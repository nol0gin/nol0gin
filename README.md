## Informal mathematics & fuzzy logic

miniKanren taught me to distrust the one-answer habit: a relation runs backwards and the world forks. I work the same way with fuzzy sets and many-valued logic, where truth carries a membership degree and vagueness is a feature of the world, not a bug of the language. Formal when needed (Rocq), informal when honest.

## Handmade agents & raising small neural nets

Every agent I run is handmade: the loop, the tools, the evals, and the refusal to outsource judgment to a framework. At the other end I like raising small neural nets locally, where you can watch every loss curve and read the whole model end to end. Big models are utilities; small ones are pets you actually understand.

## Philosophy & futures of AI/ML

Thacker, Laruelle and the Cybernetic Hypothesis keep me honest about where agent tech goes: prediction becomes governance, so the interesting question is not how strong the model is, but who is changed by the loop. I read the field as futures studies, not as a leaderboard.

## Anime that knew it in advance

Serial Experiments Lain described always-online identity in 1998, Ghost in the Shell asked whether a shell without a ghost is still a person, and Psycho-Pass handed sentencing to a model. Watching them today is reading yesterday's futures report. This is why I build agents with the safety chapter open.
